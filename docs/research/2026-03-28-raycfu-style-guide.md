## 1. The Ray Fu Voice

### Speaking Style (from transcripts)

**Pace:** Fast but clear. ~3-4 words per second. Never pauses for emphasis — momentum carries the viewer forward. No "um"s or "uh"s in any transcript.

**Tone:** Authoritative but casual. Not academic, not bro-y. More like a smart friend explaining something at a coffee shop. Uses contractions heavily ("it's", "you're", "don't"). Never sounds like he's reading a script even though he clearly is.

**Vocabulary level:** Intentionally mid-level. Uses technical terms (VPS, API keys, Docker, KV cache) but ALWAYS immediately explains them in plain English:
- "Use a VPS, which is a virtual private server"
- "The context optimization skill reduces your actual cost by reducing the amount of tokens that you use"

**Sentence structure:** Short declarative sentences. Almost never compound sentences. Average sentence length: 10-15 words. Example from his 560K video:
> "A Mac Mini costs $600. And has a single point of failure. Which means that if your electricity or your power goes down, then OpenClaw goes down. And you need to reboot it back up."

Each thought gets its own sentence. This is critical for pacing — it creates natural breath points and keeps viewers from checking out.

**First person vs second person:** He alternates:
- "I tested over 100 of them" (credibility — I did the work)
- "Here's what you need to know" (value — it's for YOU)
- "If you want to save it, comment [keyword]" (action — DO this)

**Energy:** Confident, slightly urgent, never chill. He speaks like what he's saying MATTERS and you'd be dumb to scroll past. Not hyped up like a YouTuber, not calm like a lecturer. It's the energy of someone saying "bro you need to hear this."

### What He Never Does
- Never says "in this video I'm going to show you" (wastes first 3 seconds)
- Never introduces himself ("Hey I'm Ray") — jumps straight to value
- Never apologizes or hedges ("I think maybe this could be useful")
- Never breaks the fourth wall about being a creator
- Never uses filler ("so basically", "you know", "like")
- Never asks a question at the start ("Did you know...?") — he TELLS

## 2. The Script Architecture (Frame by Frame)

Every video has exactly this structure. No exceptions in any of the 11 transcripts:

### Frame 1: THE HOOK (0-3 seconds)

The hook is ONE sentence that creates an **information gap** — you NEED to keep watching or you'll miss something valuable/scary/profitable.

**5 hook formulas he rotates:**

| Formula | Example | Views |
|---------|---------|-------|
| **Contrarian** ("Don't do X") | "You should NOT be getting a $600 Mac Mini" | 560K |
| **Dollar shock** | "Someone just automated $45,000 of SEO work in 20 minutes" | 194K |
| **Authority filter** | "There are 60,000 Claude skills. I tested 100. Here are the only ones that matter." | 159K |
| **Fear/urgency** | "Pay attention because this could save you your job" | 11.3K |
| **Free + social proof** | "Claude is giving away a free certification that Deloitte pays thousands for" | 613K |

**The #1 pattern across ALL hooks: specificity.** Never "a lot of money" — always "$82,000." Never "many skills" — always "60,000 skills." Never "some companies" — always "Deloitte." The specific number/name is what makes people stop scrolling.

### Frame 2: THE BRIDGE (3-6 seconds)

A single sentence that transitions from hook to value. Usually includes a **save/watch CTA**:
- "Save this if you're planning to build an app in the future"
- "Save this video because here's how to build one in 30 minutes"
- "Here's why." (shortest bridge — works when hook is strong enough)

### Frame 3: THE BODY (6-50 seconds)

The actual value delivery. Always follows one of these structures:

**Structure A: Rapid-fire list**
> Tool 1 for X. Tool 2 for Y. Tool 3 for Z.
One tool every 2-3 seconds. No commentary, just name + one-line description. Used in: vibecoding stack (138K), 8 Claude skills (159K).

**Structure B: Numbered steps**
> Step 1. Do this. Step 2. Do that. Step 3. The most important...
Always teases one step as "the most important" to keep people watching through the list. Used in: Claude co-work (312K), Anthropic cert (613K).

**Structure C: Story → System → Result**
> Someone did X. Here's how it works. [Technical explainer]. The edge is Y.
Used for third-party stories: NBA betting (213K), dog cancer (92K). Opens with someone else's result, explains the system, makes it feel accessible.

**Structure D: Problem → Wrong solution → Right solution**
> People are doing X. That's wrong because Y. Instead, do Z.
The contrarian structure. Used in: VPS vs Mac Mini (560K), API keys (54.5K). Creates an enemy (the wrong approach) and positions him as the guide to the right one.

### Frame 4: THE CTA (last 5-8 seconds)

Always a **two-part CTA:**
1. **Comment keyword CTA:** "Comment [keyword] and I'll send you the guide"
2. **Follow CTA:** "Follow for more tech and AI"

The comment keyword changes per video. He NEVER uses the same keyword twice:
- "claw" / "co-work" / "test" / "fish" / "skill" / "job" / "story" / "web"

**Why this matters:** The keyword CTA is the secret weapon. It:
- Gets thousands of comments (7.8K on the cert reel)
- Instagram's algorithm sees high comments = high engagement = push to more people
- Triggers DM automation that delivers a lead magnet
- Captures email/contact for future marketing

## 3. Visual Style

### Camera Setup
- **Framing:** Head and shoulders, centered. Clean background (looks like apartment).
- **Lighting:** Good natural or ring light. Face is well-lit, no shadows.
- **Eye line:** Looks directly at camera lens. Never looks away. This creates intimacy.
- **Distance:** Close-up — fills ~60% of frame with his head/shoulders.

### Text/Captions
- **Auto-captions** always on (critical for sound-off viewers — most of IG/TikTok is sound-off)
- Some videos have additional **text overlays** for emphasis
- No fancy graphics, no B-roll transitions, no screen recordings in most videos

### Production Value
- **Deliberately low production.** No intro animation, no logo, no transitions. Just him talking.
- This is intentional — high production feels like ads. Low production feels like a friend.
- The only "production" is good lighting and clean audio.

### Audio
- **Recent videos:** "remy's original sound" (someone else's audio he's using as a template sound)
- **Older videos:** "Rafael Krux's Classical Cello Solo (Acoustic)" — royalty-free classical
- **Never uses trending sounds** — this is unusual. Most TikTokers chase trending audio. He doesn't.
- His voice IS the content. The background music is barely audible.
