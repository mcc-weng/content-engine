# Alex Hormozi -- Style Guide

**Purpose:** Voice, format, and production patterns for creating Hormozi-influenced content.
**Based on:** 7 transcripts, 6 analyses, social post data (120 IG posts, 179 X posts)
**Compiled:** 2026-03-31

---

## Voice & Tone

**Core energy:** Direct, confident, educational. He sounds like a coach who's already made the money and genuinely wants to teach you. Not hype, not motivational -- instructional with conviction.

**Key voice traits:**
- **No hedging.** Never "I think maybe" or "this could potentially." Always "Here's how it works" and "This is what we found."
- **Strategic profanity.** Uses "shit," "bro," occasionally stronger words -- but always for emphasis or relatability, never shock value. "The only litmus test is that you've posted something recently that's not shit."
- **Anti-guru positioning.** Explicitly distances himself from motivational speakers. "I'm not trying to motivate you, I'm trying to educate you." Admits past mistakes freely ("I told clients to stop making content... I was partially right and partially wrong").
- **Teacher energy.** "Let me explain how this works." "If you want to write something down..." "Let me learn you some stuff." He's the experienced friend who pulls you aside and breaks it down.
- **Casual authority.** Credentials stated as facts, never boasts. "$150 million a year portfolio" dropped mid-sentence as context, not the point. "Having worked with thousands of businesses now, I can tell you..."
- **Colloquial language.** Uses contractions, slang, conversational asides. "People went nuts for this." "Bro, it is worth your time if I give you a trillion dollars." Never sounds scripted despite clear structure.
- **Self-deprecating where strategic.** Admits he's not Andy Versailles, Gary Vee, or Grant Cardone. Shares stories where he was wrong (9 businesses and broke, the supplement company mistake). Vulnerability is always in service of the lesson.

## Speaking Style

### Pace & Energy
- **Variable pace.** Slows down for key concepts ("If you want to write something down: Proof, Promise, Plan"), speeds up for lists and examples. Not monotone -- energy rises and falls with the content's emotional weight.
- **Slight urgency without hype.** Speaks like what he's saying matters and you'd be losing money by not listening. But never screams or forces excitement.
- **Conversational, not performative.** Sounds like a one-on-one conversation at a whiteboard, even when presenting to thousands.

### Word Choice Patterns
- **Operational precision.** Defines terms concretely: "I would define influence as high likelihood of compliance with requests." Redefines common words: "Work equals outputs, not effort."
- **Business-specific vocabulary.** "Enterprise value," "throughput," "arbitrage," "CPMs," "RPM" -- but always explained in context.
- **Short declarative sentences mixed with longer explanatory ones.** He alternates between punchy one-liners and multi-sentence explanations.

### Signature Phrases & Verbal Tics
- "Let me learn you some stuff"
- "Here's the thing..." / "The thing is..."
- "And so..." (transition phrase he uses constantly)
- "I'll break it down" / "Let me break this down"
- "Now, some people are like..." (sets up objections to knock down)
- "Which is..." (inline clarification)
- "All right?" (soft check-in with audience)
- "That's the point here."
- "And I'm saying this with the pain of experience"
- "Dollars to donuts"

### Repetition & Callbacks
- **Definition anchoring.** Redefines a word at the start, then uses that redefinition as a recurring anchor throughout. Example: redefines "work" as "outputs, not effort," then every later point references that definition.
- **Running examples.** Introduces a single example early (certification question, sales call scenario), then returns to it repeatedly as complexity builds. The audience tracks one story through multiple layers.
- **Memorable metaphors recycled.** "The woman in the red dress," "the grass is greener -- it's lined with shit," "be like smoke." Once introduced, he references these shorthand throughout.
- **Structural repetition.** "The more you give, the more you grow. The more you take, the more you shrink." Parallel construction for memorability.

## Script Architecture

### Long-Form Structure (YouTube, Keynotes)

```
1. CREDIBILITY OPENER (0-60 sec)
   Proof stack: specific numbers, specific results, specific timeframe.
   "$4M spent, 35,000 pieces of content, 1,000 hours on camera, 40 months."
   "Out-earned CEOs of McDonald's, Ikea, Ford, Motorola, Yahoo combined."

2. PROMISE (what you'll get)
   "I learned six things that made money and grew my brand
    that you can use today. I'm gonna save you $4M and three and a half years."

3. PLAN (set expectations)
   "There are six changes and I'm going to walk through them."
   "I'm going to break down the only four ways..."

4. FRAMEWORK DELIVERY (the meat)
   Named frameworks with clear numbering.
   Each point: principle -> why it matters -> concrete example -> common mistake.
   Heavy use of data and specific numbers rather than abstract advice.
   Acknowledges counterarguments directly mid-stream.

5. LIVE DEMONSTRATION or CASE STUDY (the memorable moment)
   The offer-building demo, the data comparison, the straw man roleplay.
   Shows the principle in action, not just describes it.

6. SUMMARY + CTA
   Clean restatement of all key points.
   CTA embedded naturally: "If you're a business doing $3M+, go to acquisition.com."
   Anti-guru closer: "If you don't execute anything, you learned nothing."
```

**The PPP intro formula (his own framework):** Lead with whichever of Proof, Promise, Plan is strongest for that piece. For business education, Proof is usually #1 because the default question is "why should I listen to you?"

### Short-Form Structure (Reels, TikTok, Shorts)

```
1. HOOK (0-3 sec): Contrarian claim, specific number, or reframe
2. SETUP (3-8 sec): Quick context or "here's why"
3. PAYOFF (8-45 sec): The framework, list, or insight
4. CTA (last 5 sec): "Comment [keyword]" or "Follow for more"
```

Short-form is almost always one of:
- **Text-on-screen one-liner** (image or reel with text overlay)
- **Talking head clip** extracted from long-form
- **Carousel** with framework/list (7% of IG posts but 2.4x higher engagement)

### How Long-Form Differs from Short-Form
- **Long-form:** Multiple frameworks stacked, progressive complexity, audience interaction, extensive proof. The full system.
- **Short-form:** One idea, one punchline, one takeaway. No preamble. The sharpest single insight from the system.
- **Repurposing flow:** Long-form keynote -> extract best 30-60 second clips -> add text overlay -> post as reels/shorts. Also: write one-liner -> post as tweet, carousel, and text-on-screen reel simultaneously.

## Hook Templates

Extracted from transcripts and top-performing posts. Each formula with examples:

### 1. Contrarian Confession
Admit you were wrong, then pivot to the hard-won insight.
> "Six years ago, I told clients they should stop making content... I was partially right and partially wrong."
> "I had 9 businesses and I was broke."

### 2. Credential + Promise Stack
Open with proof, immediately promise value.
> "We got 32.7 million views on YouTube this month. Here's how."
> "In 2016 I had $1,000 to my name, sleeping on a gym floor. Nine years later, I broke the Guinness World Record. In this video, I'm gonna show you how."

### 3. Contrarian Reframe (One-Liner)
Flip a common belief using parallel structure.
> "Buy shit from your friends' businesses and try to get free shit from strangers. Not the other way around." (156K likes)
> "Rich people buy time. Poor people buy stuff. Ambitious people buy skills. Lazy people buy distraction." (26K likes)
> "Losers become losers by being afraid of losing."

### 4. Identity Statement
Make the reader self-identify.
> "You don't have to be the smartest to win. You just have to be the last man standing."
> "Customers become friends faster than friends become customers."
> "The world will reward you in proportion to your courage, not your intellect."

### 5. Data/Number Hook
Lead with a specific, surprising number.
> "$4 million spent, 35,000 pieces of content, 40 months. Here are the six things that actually worked."
> "30.44% of people who finish their first month make their first dollar online."
> "75% of our top-performing videos emphasized message. 75% of our bottom performers emphasized production."

### 6. Common Belief Destruction
Name the thing everyone believes, then demolish it.
> "There was a theory that media works like a funnel... But in our experience, it works much more like this."
> "The 'curiosity gap' advice from marketing is wrong for education."
> "Everyone thinks the grass is greener on the other side. It is -- it's lined with shit."

### 7. Problem Identification
Name the pain the audience already feels.
> "The issue most people struggle with is you're making not enough content and the types of content aren't attracting the right customers."
> "Some of you guys are chasing views when what you want is prospects."

### 8. Challenge/Dare
Directly challenge the viewer to act.
> "If you do nothing new but only what you already know you should be doing, you will make more money in the next 12 months than you can imagine."
> "I want you to look at your number one piece of content from last year. Just post it again."

### 9. Definition Reframe
Take a word everyone uses and give it a new, precise definition.
> "I would define influence as high likelihood of compliance with requests."
> "Work equals outputs, not effort. Not hours. Not force times distance."
> "Hardest working person means highest output per unit time, not longest hours."

### 10. The "What If" Inversion
Show how to guarantee failure, then flip it.
> "How to guarantee staying poor: do lots of different things, for short periods, never get help, make all mistakes yourself, and repeat them."

### 11. Proof by Absurdity
Use an extreme example to make the point undeniable.
> "Bro, it is worth your time if I give you a trillion dollars."
> "Elon Musk made $140 billion in 2022 -- more than Bill Gates' entire career in one year."

### 12. Story Entry
Open with a specific person/moment that pulls you in.
> "Mosey Nation, the other day I was approached by a service-based brick and mortar business..."
> "A mutual friend -- business owner doing $10M+ a year -- visited HQ and casually said he'd stopped watching my content."

## Post Templates

### One-Liner (X/Twitter, Instagram Caption, LinkedIn)
```
[Contrarian reframe or identity statement in 1-2 sentences]
```
- Parallel structure preferred ("X do Y. Z do W.")
- No hashtags on X. Minimal on IG.
- Same text cross-posted everywhere with zero adaptation.
- Examples: "The world will reward you in proportion to your courage, not your intellect."

### Carousel (Instagram -- 7% of posts but 2.4x engagement)
```
Slide 1: Bold claim or framework title (large text, dark background)
Slides 2-7: One point per slide, short text, visual hierarchy
Final slide: Summary + CTA ("Comment [keyword] for the full breakdown")
```
- Best for: frameworks, numbered lists, business breakdowns
- Higher saves than reels (higher-intent audience)

### Reel / Short (Instagram, TikTok, YouTube Shorts -- 93% of IG posts)
```
[Hook one-liner on screen] (0-3 sec)
[Talking head delivery of insight] (3-45 sec)
[CTA text overlay] (last 5 sec)
```
- Text takes up 70-80% of frame when language-heavy
- Minimal editing, no fancy transitions
- One idea per reel, never two

### Long-Form Video (YouTube)
```
[PPP Intro: Proof + Promise + Plan] (0-90 sec)
[Framework with numbered sections] (bulk)
[Live demo or case study] (memorable peak)
[Summary + natural CTA] (close)
```
- Clear, descriptive title (not curiosity-gap clickbait)
- Thumbnail shows exactly what the video delivers
- Introduce yourself and your credentials every time

### Comment-Trigger Post (Any Platform, ~30% of Posts)
```
[Value content -- framework, insight, or tip]

Comment "[KEYWORD]" and I'll send you [specific free resource].
```
- Drives 400-1500+ comments vs 50-100 organic
- Keyword changes every post (MAP, SCALE, LEVERAGE, etc.)
- The resource is real and delivered via DM automation

## CTA Patterns

### Comment Triggers (Primary Growth Lever)
- "Comment 'MAP' and I'll send you our free scaling roadmap"
- Always offers something specific and valuable
- Appears in ~30% of posts
- Consistently drives 10-30x more comments than organic

### Follow CTA
- "Follow for more business education" -- embedded in caption, never a separate ask
- Simple, direct, no pleading

### Mid-Video Product Plug
- Woven naturally into the teaching: "If you're a business doing $3M or more, go to acquisition.com"
- Demonstrates the tactic he's teaching while executing it (meta-level)
- Never breaks the educational flow

### Anti-Salesy Positioning
- "I'm not selling you anything in this video"
- Gives away the full framework for free, then points to deeper resources
- "The more you give, the more you grow. The more you take, the more you shrink."
- CTAs feel like generous offers, not asks

### End-of-Video Funnel
- Points to longer compilation videos ("4-hour sales training")
- Free video -> binge session -> website -> application
- Never hard-sells on camera

## Visual Style

### YouTube Thumbnails
- **Clear, descriptive text.** Exactly what the video delivers. "Blueprint: $0 to $1M" outperforms vague money imagery.
- **His face** with an expressive reaction (confidence, intensity, slight smirk)
- **High contrast** text overlays -- readable at thumbnail size
- **No curiosity-gap clickbait.** The thumbnail IS the promise.

### On-Screen Text
- **Language-heavy frames.** Text can take up 70-80% of the frame and outperform face-on-camera content.
- **Charts, data visualizations, and clarifying text** -- not decorative effects.
- From "visual effects" to "visualizing data." From distracting to enhancing.
- **Words on screen match what he's saying** -- reinforces retention.

### Editing Approach
- **Minimal production, maximum message.** 75% of top-performing content emphasized language over production. 75% of bottom performers emphasized effects over message.
- **No fancy transitions, no B-roll montages, no intro animations.** Just him talking with supporting text/data.
- **Background color changes and visual effects don't help anyone learn** -- cut them.
- Standard talking-head format for most content. Clean background, good lighting.
- **Pre-production over post-production.** Spending 25% of former post-production time on pre-research eliminates 90-95% of editing and enables 4x output at higher quality.

### Platform-Specific Visual Notes
- **Instagram:** 93% reels (talking head + text overlay), 7% carousels (text slides, dark backgrounds)
- **X/Twitter:** Pure text posts. No images needed for one-liners.
- **YouTube:** Talking head with supporting data/text on screen. Clear thumbnails.
- **LinkedIn:** Text posts, occasionally with a simple image.

## Anti-Patterns -- What to Avoid

### Content Strategy Mistakes
- **Chasing views over revenue.** Views from the wrong audience are worse than moderate views from the right audience. His 90-day edutainment experiment: views went 2-3x higher, ad revenue dropped 50%.
- **Curiosity-gap thumbnails.** Vague imagery that doesn't match the video content kills retention. Tell them exactly what it is.
- **Going wide on topics.** Relationship, college, food, and lifestyle content attracted non-business-owner audiences and hurt conversions. Stay narrow.
- **Prioritizing shorts over longs.** Shorts viewers watch more shorts. Long-form drives conversions. Shorts are discovery, not the funnel.
- **Inside jokes.** They put new viewers on the outside. Make every reference self-contained.
- **Assuming viewers know you.** Never skip the intro. "Warm people like reminders; cold people need the introduction."

### Voice/Tone Mistakes
- **Motivational speaker energy.** Hormozi explicitly rejects this. He educates, not motivates.
- **Hedging and qualifying.** "I think maybe this could work for some people" -- this is anti-Hormozi. Be direct.
- **Sounding scripted.** Even with clear structure, delivery must feel conversational.
- **Humblebragging.** State credentials as context, not as the point. Drop the number, move on.
- **Being combative.** "It's a dance, not a fight. It's seduction, not rape." Even when disagreeing, maintain respect and curiosity.

### Production Mistakes
- **Overproduction.** Changing background colors, adding effects, fancy transitions -- these clustered in bottom-performing content.
- **Spending on post-production instead of pre-production.** Research before filming, not editing after.
- **Making content for your team instead of your audience.** The "for us to for you" shift -- if your media team loves it but your target customer stopped watching, you've drifted.

### Tactical Mistakes
- **No CTA.** Without comment triggers, you're leaving 10-30x engagement on the table.
- **Same content type all the time.** Mix one-liners (high engagement) with frameworks (high saves) with personal stories (high virality).
- **Not reposting hits.** Your audience has grown. New followers haven't seen your greatest hits. Repost them.

## Style Checklist

Quick reference before creating any piece of content in this style:

- [ ] **Opens with proof, promise, or plan** -- never "hey guys, in this video..."
- [ ] **States credentials as context** -- one sentence, specific numbers, then move on
- [ ] **One clear framework or insight** -- named, numbered, structured
- [ ] **Every concept has a concrete example** -- not abstract advice, real scenarios with real numbers
- [ ] **Acknowledges counterarguments** -- "I can hear the thoughts coming up, let me address those"
- [ ] **Language over production** -- text, data, and words dominate the frame, not effects
- [ ] **Conversational tone** -- contractions, colloquial language, strategic profanity
- [ ] **No hedging** -- direct statements, not qualified opinions
- [ ] **Comment-trigger CTA** -- "Comment [KEYWORD] for [specific resource]" on at least 30% of posts
- [ ] **Introduces self every time** -- assume every viewer is a stranger seeing you for the first time
