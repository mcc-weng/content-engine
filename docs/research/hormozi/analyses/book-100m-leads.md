# Book Analysis: $100M Leads -- Alex Hormozi

**Book:** $100M Leads: How to Get Strangers to Want to Buy Your Stuff
**Author:** Alex Hormozi
**Published:** 2023
**Part of:** Acquisition.com $100M Series (sequel to $100M Offers)

## Core Thesis

Every business has the same problem: not enough customers. And every customer starts as a stranger. The solution is advertising -- letting people know about your stuff. There are only four ways to do it (the "Core Four"), and once you master them yourself, you unlock four types of "lead getters" -- other people and systems that do advertising for you. The book provides the exact playbooks Hormozi used to generate 20,000+ leads per day across 16 industries, distilled into repeatable frameworks anyone can implement starting with zero budget.

## Key Definitions

- **Lead:** A person you can contact -- phone number, email, social handle, physical address. That is it.
- **Engaged Lead:** A person who shows interest in the stuff you sell. This is the true output of advertising. "How do I know if they're interested? Make them an offer."
- **Core Offer:** The main product/service you sell for money.
- **Lead Magnet:** A complete solution to a narrow problem, given away for free to create engaged leads.
- **Advertising:** Letting people know about the stuff you sell. Nothing more.

## Key Frameworks

### 1. The Core Four -- Lead Generation Methods

Hormozi argues there are only four ways to let people know about your stuff. Everything else is a variation of these:

| Method | Cost | Speed | You Need |
|--------|------|-------|----------|
| Warm Outreach | Free | Slow | Existing contacts |
| Cold Outreach | Free | Slow | Lists + effort |
| Posting Free Content | Free | Slow, then fast | Time + consistency |
| Running Paid Ads | Money | Fast | Budget + skill |

**Key insight:** You only need ONE method to build a business. Master one before adding another. Most people fail because they spread thin across all four instead of going deep on one.

**Which to start with:**
- Time-rich, money-poor --> Warm outreach first, then content
- Money-rich, time-poor --> Paid ads first
- Everyone --> Add lead getters (Section IV) once one method works

### 2. The Lead Magnet Framework

A lead magnet is not a crappy PDF. It is a complete solution to a narrow problem that is so valuable people would pay for it. You give it away for free in exchange for contact information.

**Why it works:** When you solve one problem, a new problem reveals itself. If your core offer solves that new problem, the lead magnet creates its own demand for your paid product.

**The 7 Steps to Create a Lead Magnet:**

1. **Identify the problem and audience.** Pick a narrow, specific problem your target market has. Create different lead magnets for different stages of the customer lifecycle.

2. **Figure out how to solve it.** Three types of solutions:
   - Reveal the problem (they did not know they had it)
   - Solve a recurring problem (they will need you again)
   - Provide one step of a multi-step process (they need your help for the rest)

3. **Choose a delivery mechanism:**
   - Software (tools, calculators, templates)
   - Information (guides, checklists, video trainings)
   - Services (free consultation, audit, assessment)
   - Physical products (samples, free trials)

4. **Name and test it.** Test in this priority order: (1) Headline, (2) Image, (3) Subheadline. The name matters more than the content for getting opt-ins.

5. **Make it easy to consume.** Reduce friction. If nobody consumes it, nobody gets value, nobody buys your core offer.

6. **Make it exceptionally good.** "99% won't buy, but they'll damage your reputation if you provide fluff." Your lead magnet IS your first impression. People judge your brand through free offerings and share recommendations.

7. **Make it easy to take the next step.** Add a clear CTA on the thank-you page. Include an upsell or next action. Two elements of an effective CTA: (1) What to do, (2) Why now (scarcity, urgency, deadline).

**The philosophy:** "A person who pays with their time now is more likely to pay with their money later."

### 3. Warm Outreach

This is the simplest, most accessible method. You contact people who already know you -- phone contacts, social media followers, old colleagues, friends, family.

**The Playbook:**

**Step 1: Build your list.** Write down every person you know. Scroll through your phone contacts, social media connections, old email threads. Hormozi says most people know 100+ people.

**Step 2: Use the ACA Framework (Acknowledge, Compliment, Ask):**
- **A -- Acknowledge:** Reference something specific about them. "Cool, my dad is also an accountant."
- **C -- Compliment:** Give a sincere, subtle compliment related to the acknowledgment. "You must be very detail-oriented."
- **A -- Ask:** Transition naturally to what you sell. "Does sitting all day prevent you from exercising?" (if you are in fitness)

**Step 3: The warm outreach script template:**
- Describe the prospect's struggles and desired outcome
- Explain your limited capacity ("I'm taking on five case studies")
- Show your guarantee and/or social proof
- Ask for referrals, not just direct sales
- Use humor to reduce awkwardness: "Does anyone you hate come to mind?"

**Step 4: Follow up.** Contact a minimum of 100 people per day for the next 100 days. Follow up at least twice with each person. Most people dramatically underestimate how many touches it takes.

**Key insight:** You do not need them to buy. You need them to refer. Most people who receive referrals become interested in your offer. Warm leads already trust you, so you can often skip the lead magnet and go straight to the core offer.

### 4. Cold Outreach

Cold outreach means contacting strangers -- people who have never heard of you. This includes cold email, cold DMs, cold calls, and cold text messages.

**The Two Barriers:**
1. They do not know you (solved by personalization)
2. They do not trust you (solved by delivering big, fast value)

**The Playbook:**

**Step 1: Get targeted lists.**
- Use scraping software/tools
- Buy from lead brokers/databases
- Manual scraping from directories, LinkedIn, Google Maps
- Search "outbound leads scraping tool" or "database lead scraping"

**Step 2: Craft your message.**
- Keep messages below a third-grade reading level. Hormozi tested this and found a 50% increase in response rates with simpler language.
- Do not "tickle interest" -- blow their minds with genuine, paid-level value in under 30 seconds.
- Personalize every message (this is what separates cold from spam).

**Step 3: Deliver value first.**
- Before asking for anything, give them something valuable -- a free audit, a personalized analysis, an insight they did not have.
- The goal is to make them think "If the free stuff is this good, what's the paid stuff like?"

**Step 4: Volume.**
- Contact a minimum of 100 people per day for the next 100 days.
- Cold outreach is a numbers game. The more people you reach out to, the more engaged leads you get.
- Most people fail because they send 10 messages, get no replies, and quit.

**Cold email structure:** Call Out + Value + Call to Action.

### 5. Content Strategy -- Posting Free Content

Content is the method that starts slow but compounds over time. "Content may disappear, but your audience compounds." The audience itself is the asset, not any individual piece of content.

**Part I -- Consistency:**
- Post regularly across all platforms
- Success scales with volume: 10x content output led to 1.2M audience growth in 6 months
- Frequency beats quality when starting out

**Part II -- Content Strategy:**

**The Hook-Retain-Reward Framework:**
- **Hook:** The first 1-5 seconds. Gets attention. "People don't have shorter attention spans, they have higher standards." Your hook must pass the "is this for me?" test instantly.
- **Retain:** Keep them engaged through the middle. Use stories, pattern interrupts, and escalating value.
- **Reward:** Deliver on the promise of the hook. Leave them better off than before they consumed your content.

**Reframing Strategy:**
- Shift from instructional to personal
- "How to" becomes "How I"
- "Best way" becomes "My favorite way"
- This makes content feel authentic and less generic

**Niche Down First:** Dominate a small niche before expanding. Example: plumbing in a specific town, not "all small businesses everywhere."

**The Give-to-Ask Ratio:**
- Television: 47 minutes of content per 13 minutes of ads = ~3.5:1 ratio
- Facebook newsfeed: ~4 content posts per 1 ad = 4:1 ratio
- Your content should follow a similar pattern: give 3-4x more than you ask
- The more value you give for free, the more trust you build, the easier the sale becomes

**11 Ways to Increase Customer Confidence (use in content):**
1. Proof of your personal achievement
2. Proof others like them succeeded
3. Volume of positive reviews
4. Third-party certifications/credentials
5. Data, statistics, research
6. Expert/celebrity endorsements
7. Guarantees
8. Specificity to their exact pain
9. Live or recorded demonstrations
10. Case studies with before/after
11. Social proof and testimonials

### 6. Paid Ads

Hormozi is honest: "I've lost money more times than I've made money running paid ads." One business took a full year to make ads profitable. He recommends doing paid ads last for two reasons: (1) skills from organic channels transfer to paid, and (2) profits from organic channels fund ad testing.

**The Ad Creation Formula:** Call Out + Value + Call to Action

**Part I -- Making an Ad:**

**Targeting Strategy (progressive expansion):**
1. Start with entire audience (haystack)
2. Narrow to ideal customers (needles)
3. Pick the platform where your audience lives
4. Use platform's native targeting
5. Craft messaging that repels wrong-fit prospects
6. Expand progressively: puddle --> pond --> lake --> ocean

**Call Out (most important element):**
- First 5 seconds determine success or failure
- Get eyes/ears long enough for "this is for me" recognition
- Match audience demographics visually and linguistically

**Value Elements (show and tell):**
- Dream outcome (maximum benefit)
- Nightmare scenario (cost of inaction)
- Current slow trajectory vs. faster path with your solution
- Effort required without you vs. with you

**Landing Page Alignment:**
- Match ad language and visuals exactly on the landing page
- Leverage consistency principle (Cialdini): "Now that you clicked A, do B to maximize A"
- Mismatched landing pages kill conversion

**Part II -- Money Stuff:**

**Key Metrics:**
- LTGP (Lifetime Gross Profit): Total customer spending minus cost of delivery
- CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost): Total ad spend divided by customers acquired
- Target: minimum 3:1 LTGP-to-CAC ratio
- Many agencies operate at 2:1 (50% of revenue goes to acquisition) -- this is barely surviving

**Critical insight:** Low CAC often means a weak business model, not good ads. Winners compete on LTGP, not CAC. If your product delivers more value, customers stay longer and spend more, which means you can afford to spend more to acquire them.

**Testing Framework:**
- Spend $100 on each of 10 different ads ($1,000 total)
- Find the winner (e.g., one returns $500 on $100 spend)
- Then "100x down" -- pour $10,000 into the winner
- Top 0.1% of advertisers test 11x more variations than average

**Cash Flow Strategy:**
- Recover customer acquisition + fulfillment costs within 30 days
- Pay off credit card
- Repeat
- This strategy scaled portfolio companies past $1M/month in first 12 months without outside funding

**Warning:** Ads require weekly attention minimum. "You ignored the ads for two weeks!?" is how you lose $950K without generating $1M in sales. Platform interfaces are complicated -- get a consultant or watch tutorials before starting.

### 7. Affiliates & Referrals -- Getting Other People to Advertise for You

This is Section IV of the book: "Lead Getters." Once you can generate leads yourself, you unlock the ability to get others to do it for you.

**The Four Lead Getters:**

**1. Customer Referrals / Word of Mouth:**
- Treat referrals as a formal offer with a clear value proposition, not a casual afterthought
- Offer an incentive equal to your average CAC to the referrer, the referred friend, or both
- Ask customers who they want to bring, then request direct three-way introductions (not vague "tell your friends")
- Create tiered referral bonuses: VIP access, courses, tokens, status, training, merchandise, premium support
- Guarantee strategy: Note what successful customers did, then guarantee results to new customers conditional on repeating those same actions

**2. Employees:**
- "If you trade forty hours of doing for four hours managing, you work thirty-six hours less."
- Hire people to do the Core Four methods for you
- Three-week rule: entry-level employees get three weeks to prove suitability
- Two-step training: (1) Document -- create checklists, (2) Demonstrate -- execute in front of them
- Focus on ability to follow directions during training, not results
- Fast feedback cycles accelerate skill development

**3. Agencies:**
- Do not believe you will never need to learn their platform
- Partner for a 6-month learning engagement
- Pay premium for transparent decision-making breakdowns
- Hire a "good enough" agency first to learn fundamentals
- Then upgrade to an elite agency to maximize the platform
- Transition to lower-cost consulting once you understand the platform yourself
- Hormozi paid one agency owner $750/hour for hands-on platform training

**4. Affiliates and Partners:**
- Independent businesses with their own audience who promote your stuff for money, free products, or both
- Ideal affiliate: business owner with a warm audience matching your customer profile

**Scaling math:**
- Direct sales: 10 customers/month x $10,000 = $100K/month = $1.2M/year
- Affiliate model: Sign 10 affiliates/month, each brings 1 customer = adds $100K/month, growing perpetually = $7.8M in year one

**Three-tier commission structure:**
- Tier 1 (25% of CAC): e.g., $10/order -- anyone can join
- Tier 2 (50% of CAC): e.g., $20/order -- activated at 2+ orders/month
- Tier 3 (100% of CAC): e.g., $40/order -- activated at 5+ orders/month, resets monthly

**Affiliate value proposition (pitch to potential affiliates):**
- Make more money from existing customers
- Get additional leads beyond your current offering
- High likelihood of success (their audience already wants the product)
- No product building, delivery, or support required
- Start selling tomorrow

**Launch strategy -- Whisper, Tease, Shout:**
1. **Whisper:** Post regular content, reach out to friends and existing list, share behind-the-scenes, show drafts and iterations
2. **Tease:** Reveal the product, announce launch date publicly, show elements of value, build curiosity
3. **Shout:** Provide specific CTA instructions, emphasize bonuses/scarcity/urgency/guarantees, target first movers, maximum exposure

### 8. The Rule of 100

This is Hormozi's minimum effective dose for lead generation:

- **Warm/Cold Outreach:** Contact 100 people per day
- **Content:** Spend 100 minutes per day creating content
- **Paid Ads:** Spend $100 per day on advertising

Do this for 100 days straight. Most people never come close to this volume, which is why most people fail. The Rule of 100 is not a suggestion -- it is the minimum to get meaningful data on what is working.

### 9. The More, Better, New Framework

When you want more leads, you have exactly three options:

1. **More:** Do more of what is already working. Most people stop here too early. Increase volume until something breaks and you hit a constraint.

2. **Better:** Fix the constraint. Find where leads are dropping off and optimize that specific part. Improve conversion rates, response rates, or follow-up speed. Do this while maintaining current volume.

3. **New:** Only after exhausting "more" and "better," introduce something new:
   - New ad placements on a known platform
   - Known placements on a new platform
   - New placements on a newly mastered platform
   - An entirely new Core Four activity

**Testing cadence:** One test per week per platform.
**Timeline expectation:** 3-6 months to crack a new lead source.

Most people jump to "new" immediately because it is exciting. This is a mistake. There is almost always more juice to squeeze from what is already working.

## Application to Starting an Agency (Zero Budget)

Here is how Mike can apply these frameworks specifically to getting his first clients with no money:

### Week 1-2: Foundation
1. **Define the core offer.** What specific problem do you solve, for whom, and what is the outcome? Use the $100M Offers value equation.
2. **Create a lead magnet.** A free audit, assessment, or mini-service that demonstrates your competence. Example: "Free AI Workflow Audit -- I'll identify 3 processes you can automate this week." Make it specific enough to be valuable, narrow enough to complete in 30 minutes.

### Week 3-12: Warm Outreach Blitz (Primary)
3. **Build the 100-person list.** Scroll through every contact on your phone, LinkedIn, Instagram, old email threads. Write down everyone who runs a business or works at one.
4. **Use ACA framework daily.** Acknowledge something specific, compliment sincerely, ask a relevant question that leads to your offer.
5. **Hit the Rule of 100.** Contact 100 people per day. You will run out of warm contacts quickly, which naturally pushes you to cold outreach.
6. **Ask for referrals, not sales.** "Do you know anyone who [has the problem you solve]? I'm taking on 5 case studies at no charge to build my portfolio."
7. **Follow up relentlessly.** Most people need 3-7 touches before responding.

### Week 3-12: Content (Secondary, parallel)
8. **Spend 100 minutes per day creating content.** Document what you are learning and doing. Use "How I" framing, not "How to."
9. **Niche down hard.** Do not try to be everything to everyone. Pick one industry vertical and one platform.
10. **Give-to-ask ratio of 4:1.** Four valuable posts for every one that mentions your services.

### Month 3+: Scale
11. **Formalize referrals.** Once you have a few clients, create a referral incentive. Offer a month free, a bonus service, or a cash incentive equal to your CAC.
12. **Add cold outreach.** Use the warm outreach skills you have built. Personalize everything. Keep messages at third-grade reading level. Deliver value in under 30 seconds.
13. **Consider paid ads only after organic is profitable.** Use organic content that performed well as ad creative. Start with $100/day.

### Key Principles for Zero-Budget Start:
- **Do warm outreach first.** It is free, it works immediately, and it builds the skills you need for everything else.
- **Your lead magnet IS your sales pitch.** If you give away something genuinely valuable, the sale happens naturally.
- **Volume solves most problems.** If you are not getting results, you are probably not doing enough volume.
- **One channel at a time.** Master warm outreach before adding content. Master content before adding cold outreach.
- **Case studies are currency.** Do the first few projects for free or cheap to build undeniable proof.
