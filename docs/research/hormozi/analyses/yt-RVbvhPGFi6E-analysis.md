# Analysis: New Sales Method from 4000+ Closes

**Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVbvhPGFi6E
**Key Topic:** The AAA (Acknowledge, Associate, Ask) reframing framework -- the skill that separates top closers from the rest

## Core Frameworks

### 1. The 3A (AAA) Reframing Framework

The central methodology. Reframing is the one to three sentences you say after a prospect says anything but yes, before entering your close. Without it, objection handling feels combative. With it, you can say almost anything while maintaining rapport.

- **A -- Acknowledge:** Repeat back what the prospect said in your own words. Two benefits: (1) the prospect feels heard and believes you're actively listening, (2) it buys you 2-3 seconds to process and plan your next move. Example: "So you're curious about what our certifications are."

- **A -- Associate:** Connect their question or objection to behavior exhibited by your best, most successful customers. This reframes their step-away-from-buying as actually a step-toward-buying. Example: "That's actually a question that a lot of our best customers ask." Three association subtypes below (straw men).

- **A -- Ask:** After reframing, ask a question that attacks the frame of their original question. Never answer a question you don't know the right answer to -- instead, ask questions about their question. Example: "Which certifications are you looking for specifically?"

**Why it works:** The person asking questions controls the conversation. As soon as you start answering the prospect's questions, they become judge, jury, and executioner of whether your answers are good enough. AAA keeps you in the driver's seat.

### 2. The "Be Like Smoke" Principle

You can't catch smoke. Instead of meeting objections head-on (combative), you sidestep with questions. Always shifting, always engaging, but never giving them a solid wall to push against. This lets you ask for the sale as many times as needed without decreasing rapport.

**Core insight:** The best closers don't have better answers -- they ask for the sale more times than anyone else. But asking more times only works if each ask doesn't erode rapport. AAA is the mechanism that preserves rapport through repeated asks.

### 3. Three Types of Straw Men (Deep Dive on "Associate")

When you must deliver a tough truth or break a flawed belief, use a third-party character (straw man/foil) so you're not insulting the prospect directly:

1. **The Earlier Conversation:** "I had someone earlier today ask the same question. Can I share what I shared with them?" Now you can be brutally honest about that other person's situation without directing it at the prospect.

2. **The Successful Past Customer:** "That actually makes you just like Sarah, who had a similar plumbing business and crushed it with us. She asked this same question too." Associates the prospect with a winner, then handles the objection through Sarah's story.

3. **The Authority Appeal:** "It's funny you asked that -- Alex [CEO] just told me this thing earlier today. Can I share it?" You become the messenger, not the authority. The prospect hears wisdom from a credible source without feeling talked down to by the salesperson.

**Why straw men matter:** Especially critical for younger salespeople selling to older/more experienced prospects. A 22-year-old can't put on a "coach hat" with a 45-year-old $10M business owner. But they can relay what the CEO said, or share a story about another customer.

### 4. Conviction-Based Selling Frame (The Time Travel Analogy)

Imagine you could travel back in time in a different body and had to convince your old self to buy NVIDIA stock 10 years ago. You'd have absolute conviction AND absolute empathy. You wouldn't be combative -- you'd genuinely want to understand their concerns because you KNOW this is the right decision.

**The reframe:** If you genuinely believe your product helps qualified prospects, every objection is just a concern to understand and resolve -- not an attack to defend against. You don't close sales by being right. You close sales by making the prospect right.

## Actionable Tactics

1. **Never ask "Do you have any questions?"** -- This is literally asking the prospect to take control of the conversation and generate objections. Train this out of every salesperson immediately.

2. **When you don't know the answer, ask more questions about their question.** This is the "ultra mega hack" -- you never need to be caught off guard. "Which certifications specifically?" / "Why those certifications?" / "What are you trying to solve with this?"

3. **Use the mechanic/surgeon analogy when pressed to answer.** "You're asking me what's wrong with your car -- it would be unethical for me to answer without looking under the hood first." Then seal it: "If anyone gives you an answer on the first call without doing an assessment, run the other way."

4. **Give labels early, cash them in at the close.** When someone asks a smart question, label them: "You seem like a really rational person." Later at the close, reference the label: "As a rational person, you can see why this makes sense." People live up to labels you give them.

5. **Universal response for deep objections:** "What are you most afraid of?" -- Gets to the heart of the issue immediately. Use softer versions early ("What's your main concern?" / "What are the variables in your decision?") and escalate to "What are you afraid of?" later in the sale.

6. **Use "What would make this a no?" and "What would it take for you to say yes?"** -- These force the prospect to name specific, addressable concerns instead of hiding behind vague resistance.

7. **Physical hack: Say "Huh" with genuine curiosity.** Even on phone calls, stand/sit as if genuinely curious. Smile. Your tone changes based on body position. Keep a mirror by your sales station. "Huh, that's so interesting you'd ask that. Can I ask you a little more about that?"

8. **Put a sticky note above your camera: "Keep the human #1."** Seek to understand, not to win. The moment it feels combative, you've lost.

9. **Prospects believe almost nothing you say and almost everything they say.** Don't tell them they're a good fit -- ask questions that lead them to say "I think I'm a good fit for this." Breadcrumb them to the logical conclusion.

10. **The "I don't have time" reframe done right:** Don't say "It's not a timing thing, it's a priorities question" (even though it's true). Instead: "Totally understand. Time is tough right now. I think it's smart that you're already thinking about implementation. Our biggest success stories are people who think through the process the same way you are. So what would make it a good time?"

## Case Studies & Examples

### The Wealth Management Pitch (How Hormozi Discovered This)
A wealth manager pitched Hormozi. He tried to exit by saying "I already have a wealth advisor." The guy responded: "That's amazing. 90% of the clients we have that shift over already have a wealth advisor. So it means you already know how a lot of these things work, which means you'll be up the learning curve." Hormozi didn't buy, but the reframe was so powerful it became the seed of the entire AAA framework.

### The Certification Trap Question
A prospect asks "How many certifications do your trainers have?" -- a blind question where any answer gets judged. Instead of answering: "Which certifications are you looking for specifically?" If they name specific ones you don't have: "Why those certifications specifically?" Then: "Those are amazing certifications. We have a couple other ones -- let me tell you why we chose these rather than those." You've now answered without failing.

### The Sales Floor Save
Hormozi walked the sales floor, heard a rep struggling. The prospect wasn't sold. Hormozi grabbed the phone and asked one question: "Dude, what are you afraid of?" -- Cut straight to the heart of the issue. Demonstrates how deep-in-the-sale directness (only possible with rapport) accelerates the close.

### The Combative Closer
Hormozi was reviewing the closing manual and heard a salesperson "immediately fire back an obstacle overcome" that sounded combative. Realized the reps were skipping the reframe step -- jumping straight to the close without the AAA buffer. That's what prompted this entire training.

## Content Structure Notes

### Hook (0:00-0:30)
Opens with credibility: "over a hundred salespeople across our portfolio that does over $250 million a year." Promises a skill he's "never taught before." Sets up intrigue + authority immediately.

### Framework Setup (0:30-2:00)
Defines "reframing" with a concrete example (certification question) before revealing the framework name. Shows the problem first, then offers the solution. Classic problem-agitate-solve.

### Core Teaching (2:00-18:00)
Structured around "3A framework + 5 rules" -- gives the audience a clear mental model. Each rule builds on the last. Heavy use of live examples and roleplay-style dialogue. Every abstract concept immediately followed by "here's how it sounds."

### Embedded CTAs (Two mid-roll plugs)
1. Acquisition.com scaling workshop (after Rule 1) -- positioned as "if you like advanced sales stuff"
2. Acquisition.com careers page (after Rule 3) -- targets salespeople watching who might want to work for him. Both feel organic rather than interruptive.

### Pacing
25-minute video but feels shorter. Rapid-fire examples after each rule. Uses humor ("I hate them all too, just kidding") to break tension. Mixes theory with practical dialogue scripts so the viewer can immediately use what they learn.

### Closing CTA (24:30)
Points to a 4-hour compilation of his best sales trainings. Classic lead magnet / content funnel move -- uses this free video as a taste, then directs to a longer binge session.

### Rhetorical Devices
- **Analogies:** Lawyer in interrogation, Hollywood "I'm the one asking questions," mechanic looking under the hood, surgeon/secretary, angry customer service boat, time-traveling to convince your old self to buy stocks
- **Pattern:** Every concept introduced as (1) principle, (2) why it matters, (3) how it sounds in a real conversation, (4) what beginners get wrong
- **Callback structure:** Introduces the certification example early, returns to it repeatedly as the running thread
