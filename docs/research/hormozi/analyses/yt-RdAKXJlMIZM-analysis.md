## Core Frameworks

### 1. The Leverage Equation (Central Framework)
- **Work = Outputs** (not effort, not hours, not force x distance)
- **Outputs = Volume x Leverage**
  - Volume = number of times you move the lever (capped by time/physical ability)
  - Leverage = length of the lever / how much you get per action (uncapped)
- **Work Rate = Outputs / Time** (dollars per hour -- the objective measure of "hard work")
- Implication: "Hardest working person" = highest output per unit time, not longest hours or most effort
- Example: Elon Musk made $140B in 2022 -- more than Bill Gates' entire career in one year. That's $2B/week. By this definition, objectively the hardest worker.

### 2. Five Layers of Leverage (Sales Call Example)
Hormozi builds a progressive example of two salespeople making 100 calls/day to show leverage stacking:
1. **Skill** -- better closer wins with same volume
2. **Tools** -- automated dialer maximizes talk time
3. **List quality** -- better-targeted prospects convert higher
4. **Timing** -- right season, right time of day
5. **Offer** -- better offer converts more from same conversations
6. **Media/distribution** -- record the pitch, reach 10M people (one action, massive leverage)
7. **Team** -- 15 people on phones beats any solo performer; 1,500 is unbeatable

### 3. The Three Pillars (Presentation Structure)
1. **How making money really works** = Leverage
2. **Why new stuff is making you poor** = Focus (or lack thereof)
3. **Why better is the way** = Better = Leverage

### 4. Stages of Change / Uninformed Optimism Cycle
Five stages when pursuing something new:
1. **Uninformed Optimism** -- you hear the highlights, get excited, jump in
2. **Informed Pessimism** -- you discover all the hidden complexity and difficulty
3. **Crisis of Meaning** -- "Why am I doing this? Money doesn't matter. I should spend time with my kids." (You change your goal because you encounter difficulty)
4. **The Crash** -- most people bail here and restart at stage 1 with a new thing
5. **Informed Optimism** -- you figure out the one thing that makes it work, momentum builds
6. **Achievement** -- success; friends ask what you're doing; cycle feeds their uninformed optimism

The failure pattern: people loop stages 1-3-1-3-1-3 forever. "In video games you restart seconds later. In entrepreneurship, you restart years later."

### 5. The Anti-Wealth Formula (Inversion)
How to guarantee staying poor:
1. Do lots of different things
2. Do them for short periods of time
3. Do not get help
4. Make all mistakes yourself
5. When you make a mistake, repeat it

Flip it for wealth:
1. Do one thing for a very long period of time
2. Get help from others / observe what works
3. Only make each mistake once
4. Challenge your beliefs constantly ("What do I believe to be true that is not?")

### 6. The Woman in the Red Dress (Matrix Metaphor)
- Shiny opportunities become MORE seductive as you level up
- At $1K/mo, easy to say no to distractions
- At $10K/mo, harder. At $100K/mo, much harder
- At $20M/year, Hormozi himself failed this test (started supplement company instead of growing Gym Launch from $26M to $40M)
- The "woman in the red dress" at each level is the opportunity that matches your current revenue -- always feels justified
- You must learn to say no at every new level

### 7. Better = Leverage (Core Operating Principle)
- Better gets you more output from the same input -- that IS leverage by definition
- Better comes from boring (split tests, role plays, CRM implementation, lead follow-up)
- The entrepreneurial trap: quitting your job felt good and was rewarded, so you learned "quitting = reward." That lesson only works once -- then you must unlearn it

### 8. The Self-Coaching Mental Model
- "If I were coaching me, what would I tell me to do?"
- You have perfect information (every conversation, email, report)
- You have no competing agenda
- Half the time, what "coach you" would say is NOT what you're doing
- Conclusion: take your own advice

### 9. Compounding Vehicle Test
- "If the vehicle you are in right now does not compound with time, then change the vehicle."
- Get into something where sticking with it for an extended period makes it unreasonable that you'd be poor
- "Ordinary businesses done for extraordinary time create extraordinary results"

## Actionable Tactics

1. **Call leads within 5 minutes** -- Hormozi claims this alone triples sales. Most people know this, almost nobody does it. Requires CRM, round-robin setup, call prioritization.

2. **Split test landing pages weekly** -- not once, not when you feel like it -- every single week as a standing process.

3. **Split test emails weekly** -- same cadence, same discipline.

4. **Create new ads/creative before you need them** -- proactive, not reactive.

5. **Role play with sales team daily** -- not once at a training event. Daily reps.

6. **Role play with customer success daily** -- practice handling "I want a refund," "I hated this," "It wasn't fast enough."

7. **Take 10 more interviews for every role** -- the pain of extra interviews saves 6 months of a bad hire.

8. **Continuously improve your lead magnet** -- keep making it better rather than launching new ones.

9. **Improve your offer iteratively** -- better offer = more clicks, more signups, more shows, more closes, at higher prices.

10. **Commit to daily prospecting regardless of results** -- divorce the activity from the outcome. Identity is forged in repetitive commitment, not in wins.

11. **Make your "should-do" list** -- right now, list everything you know you should be doing in your business but aren't. That list IS your growth plan. Rank by leverage (output per unit of input) and execute top-down.

12. **Eliminate all businesses except one** -- if you have multiple businesses under ~$3M, you're not "vertically integrated," you're distracted.

13. **Fix your product before marketing it** -- negative word of mouth is invisible but devastating. If your ad costs are rising faster than CPMs, you have negative word of mouth. People are actively telling others NOT to buy from you.

## Case Studies & Examples

### Hormozi's Own Journey: 9 Businesses to 1
- At 26: owned 6 gyms, a gym launch/turnaround business, a dental agency, a chiropractor agency, and more
- Was broke despite "owning 9 companies" -- ego-stroking, not wealth-building
- Layla pointed out: the gym launch business made the most money with the least time
- Over 90 days: sold 5 gyms, shut 1 down, closed both agencies, eliminated everything else
- 12 months later: $1.5M/month from zero
- 24 months later: $4.5M/month
- Lesson: focus didn't just help -- it was the entire unlock

### Hormozi's Supplement Company Mistake
- Gym Launch was doing $26M/year
- Instead of growing to $40M, started a supplement company (the "woman in the red dress" at the $20M level)
- Gym Launch flatlined, supplement got them to $37M total
- Sold the company for big money, but admits it was "not the right move" strategically

### The Construction Buddy
- High school friend called wanting to scale his roofing business
- Also doing general contracting AND house flipping ("vertically integrated")
- Making less than $3M -- "you're not vertically integrated, you're distracted"
- Advice: "Just pick one. Why can't you build a billion dollar roofing business?"
- His objection: "I don't leave money on the table"
- Counter: the money left on the table IS the focus you're not giving the one thing

### Diane Hendricks (Forbes Richest Self-Made Woman)
- Built ABC Supply -- a roofing supply company
- Number one on Forbes self-made women list
- The construction buddy was "smarter" than her -- that's why he was still small
- Key observation about the Forbes list: ages 63-90, sources are roofing, healthcare software, retail/gas stations, trucking, Little Caesars Pizza, IT. "So sexy." The point: boring businesses + decades of focus = extraordinary wealth.

### The Prospecting Text Message
- Guy who had lost everything set an "unrealistic" business goal
- Hormozi's only advice: prospect every day, rain or shine
- After initial good months, he slowed down (felt successful from outcomes)
- Hormozi corrected: be satisfied by commitment to activity, not outcomes
- Result: surpassed his "impossible" goal by Q3

### The 7/10 vs 9.5/10 Book
- 20x more work to go from good to exceptional
- 1,000x more sales at the exceptional level
- 50x return on incremental effort -- the ultimate leverage example

### Live Offer-Building Demo ($25 Book to $100K+ Value)
- Started: "Who wants my book for $1,000?" -- 3 people stood
- Added social proof (best seller, #1 in categories for 13 months) -- a few more
- Added testimonial (reader quadrupled revenue) -- 40-50 standing
- Added autograph + personal cell phone -- two thirds standing
- Added: review 4 sales calls + rewrite scripts + drill sales team -- more
- Added: traffic expert does 11-page funnel breakdown -- ~90% standing
- Added: recruit one star player for their company
- Added: 2 hours of offer consulting
- Added: feature on his channel (10M impressions, ~$100K ad equivalent)
- Added: 3x ROI guarantee or full refund + keep all work
- Final twist: "There's only one book" (scarcity)
- Same product, same action (selling), radically different perceived value
- Demonstrates that leverage on offers is unlimited with the right value stack

## Content Structure Notes

### Format
- Live keynote/workshop presentation with audience interaction
- Heavy use of audience participation (raise hands, stand up, shout answers)
- Presentation was created the night before after impromptu conversations with attendees

### Structural Pattern
1. **Credibility opener** -- flex credentials (out-earned CEOs of McDonald's/Ikea/Ford/Motorola/Yahoo combined in his 20s, $200M portfolio revenue)
2. **Acknowledgment of reactions** -- "This will create envy in some, anger in others, skepticism in most... and inspire a select few"
3. **Three-part framework preview** -- how money works, why new is bad, why better wins
4. **Redefine a core concept** -- challenges the audience's definition of "work" and "hard work"
5. **Progressive examples** -- builds the sales call example layer by layer
6. **Story-driven teaching** -- construction buddy, magic wand friend, personal journey
7. **Emotional/identity framing** -- Matrix metaphor, courage/patience/loyalty parallel
8. **Interactive closer** -- live offer-building demonstration (the most memorable segment)
9. **Summary recap** -- restates all three pillars cleanly

### Rhetorical Techniques
- **Inversion** -- shows "how NOT to make money" before flipping to the positive
- **Audience polling** -- "who here has..." creates buy-in and self-identification
- **Progressive reveal** -- the offer demo builds suspense by layering value
- **Relatable vulnerability** -- "I had 9 businesses and was broke," "I'm saying this with the pain of experience"
- **Memorable metaphors** -- video game boss, woman in the red dress, spinning plates, grass is greener (lined with shit)
- **Definition anchoring** -- redefines "work," "learning," "patience," "loyalty" to reframe the entire conversation

### Why This Is His Most Viewed Long-Form
- It's a complete system in one talk: leverage (theory) + focus (strategy) + better (execution)
- The live offer demo is inherently viral -- dramatic, surprising, and demonstrates the lesson in real-time
- Relatable pain points (shiny object syndrome, multiple businesses, knowing what to do but not doing it)
- Strong emotional hooks: frustration with "no one can outwork me" culture, vulnerability about his own failures
- Applicable to any business at any stage -- not niche-specific
