# Post Scaffolds

6 scaffold structures for multi-platform posts. Each includes a flow, a default hook type, pacing notes, and platform-specific adaptations. Scaffolds are starting points — break them when it sounds more natural.

## Scaffold: Demo Post

**Default hook:** Numbers first
**Common alt:** Reverse contrast
**Classification:** You built/shipped something with measurable results.

**Flow:**
[hook — numbers or contrast] →
[specific result — what happened] →
[how — shortest possible version] →
[twist/surprise — the unexpected part] →
[CTA — challenge or experience-sharing]

**Pacing notes:**
- Hook can be stacked numbers (multiple short lines) — exception to the 2-line rhythm rule
- "How" section must be 1 line max. If it takes more, cut it.
- Twist is the payoff — make it land with a short punchy line

### RED Adaptation
Long-form note with headers. Title = hook (first 18 chars must contain 2 core keywords, e.g., "AI工具｜幫買方仲介省了3小時"). Body expands the demo flow to 600+ chars: use H2 headers for each stage (工具介紹 / 實際結果 / 使用方法 / 意外發現). Include screenshots or step-by-step breakdown for saves. End with engagement question ("你有在用什麼AI工具嗎？留言分享") + 5-8 hashtags. Traditional Chinese.

### Instagram Adaptation
Carousel flow:
- **Slide 1 (hook/promise):** Bold text stating the result in under 12 words. Answers "is this for me?" (e.g., "I automated 3 hours of agent work with one AI tool")
- **Slides 2-3:** The specific result — before vs. after, numbers up front
- **Slides 4-6:** The how — one step per slide, brief and visual
- **Slide 7-8:** The twist/surprise — the unexpected outcome
- **Final slide:** CTA — "Save this + try it this week" or "DM me 'DEMO' for the setup guide"
- **Caption:** 150-300 chars, English. Restate the core result + relevant hashtags.

### X Adaptation
Single tweet: compress to the result + twist in one punch, under 280 chars. Lead with numbers. Thread version: Hook tweet (numbers + result) → 2-3 body tweets (how, step by step, one per tweet) → closing tweet (surprise/lesson + question). Build-in-public formula: "Problem → Tried → Worked → Lesson."

### LinkedIn Adaptation
Expanded to 1,300-1,900 chars with aggressive line breaks (2-3 sentences per paragraph). Hook in first 210 chars: lead with the specific result (numbers, timeframe). One idea per paragraph. Structure: hook → context → the how (broken into short paragraphs) → the twist → what this means for agents. English. End with a direct question tied to the demo.

## Scaffold: Curated Insight

**Default hook:** Curiosity gap
**Common alt:** Pain point
**Classification:** You found/read/discovered something worth sharing.

**Flow:**
[hook — what you found, make them curious] →
[brief explanation — 1-2 lines max] →
[personal trial/reaction — your honest take] →
[CTA — genuine curiosity or opinion poll]

**Pacing notes:**
- Explanation must be shorter than your reaction. Your take > the info.
- If your reaction is "嗯 還好" — don't post. Find something you actually reacted to.
- Open loop in hook: hint at the discovery without naming it

### RED Adaptation
Long-form note with headers. Title hints at the discovery without revealing it (first 18 chars, e.g., "仲介必看｜這個AI功能我用了三天"). Body: context (what you found) → explanation (what it does for overseas buyer agents) → personal trial results with specifics → honest verdict. Include comparison table or numbered takeaways for saves. End with "你試過了嗎？" + hashtags. 600+ chars. Traditional Chinese.

### Instagram Adaptation
Carousel flow:
- **Slide 1 (hook/promise):** "I found something that changes how agents handle overseas buyers" — under 12 words, bold
- **Slide 2:** Brief explanation — what it is, one slide only
- **Slides 3-6:** Your trial/reaction — break the reaction into moments (first impression, surprise, what didn't work)
- **Slide 7-8:** The verdict — would you use this? Would you recommend it?
- **Final slide:** "Save this if you're testing AI tools" or "DM me [keyword] for the full list"
- **Caption:** 150-300 chars. English.

### X Adaptation
Single tweet: the discovery + your honest reaction in one punch. Thread version: Hook tweet (what you found — curiosity gap) → explanation tweet → reaction tweet (most important — your take) → closing question tweet. Your take must dominate. If the tool was underwhelming, say so.

### LinkedIn Adaptation
1,300-1,900 chars. Hook in first 210 chars: start with a specific observation about the tool/resource you found — not "I wanted to share." One idea per paragraph. Structure: hook (the find) → brief explanation → your trial in steps → honest reaction (positive AND negative) → what agents should do with this. End with "Have you tested this? What did you find?"

## Scaffold: Hot Take

**Default hook:** Bold claim
**Common alt:** Raw rant
**Classification:** You have a strong opinion others might disagree with.

**Flow:**
[hook — the opinion, no hedging] →
[evidence — from personal experience, not theory] →
["so what" — why the reader should care or act]

**Pacing notes:**
- Hook line should be under 15 characters if possible — punchy statement
- Evidence needs ONE specific detail (a number, a name, an incident)
- No conclusion paragraph. The "so what" IS the ending. Often works as integrated CTA.

### RED Adaptation
Long-form note. Title = the hot take as a punchy claim (first 18 chars, e.g., "海外買家仲介｜還不用AI就落後了"). Body: state the opinion boldly → evidence from your experience (specific numbers, client scenarios, real examples) → implication for overseas buyer agents. Structure with headers. Can be more aggressive in tone on RED — niche community appreciates insider knowledge. End with "你同意嗎？" + hashtags. 600+ chars. Traditional Chinese.

### Instagram Adaptation
Carousel flow:
- **Slide 1 (hook/promise):** The opinion as a bold declaration, under 12 words. Bold visual text.
- **Slides 2-3:** The evidence — one specific detail per slide
- **Slides 4-5:** "So what" — why this matters for agents/buyers
- **Final slide:** "Agree or disagree? Drop your take in the comments"
- **Caption:** 150-300 chars. Restate the take + "Save if you've seen this yourself." English.

### X Adaptation
Single tweet: the hot take in one line, no hedging, under 280 chars. Add evidence in reply if needed. Thread version: Hook tweet (the opinion) → evidence tweet (one specific detail) → "so what" tweet → closing tweet inviting pushback ("Disagree? Tell me why"). Build-in-public angle: "Hot take from building AI tools for agents..."

### LinkedIn Adaptation
1,300-1,900 chars. Hook in first 210 chars: lead with a contrarian opener — state something that most professionals in the space would push back on. One paragraph = one idea. Structure: hook → "here's why I believe this" → evidence (specific numbers, incidents from your work) → implication for the industry → direct invitation to disagree. Professional but confident tone. English.

## Scaffold: Personal Journey

**Default hook:** Age + result
**Common alt:** Mid-action story
**Classification:** Something happened to you that changed your thinking.

**Flow:**
[hook — result or milestone first] →
[short story — how it happened, compressed] →
[lesson — what changed, not preachy]

**Pacing notes:**
- Story section: max 3 lines. Compress ruthlessly.
- Lesson must NOT sound like advice. "我才發現..." > "你應該..."
- Vary lengths most aggressively here — story posts get monotonous fast

### RED Adaptation
Long-form note. Title = the milestone or result (first 18 chars, e.g., "買了4間澳洲房｜AI改變了我的決策方式"). Body expands the story: hook with milestone → context (who you are, why this journey) → story broken into stages with headers → lesson stated personally, not as advice. Real estate angle: your own buying experience, or your sister's agent experience, or a specific client scenario. 600+ chars. End with "你有相似的經歷嗎？" + hashtags. Traditional Chinese.

### Instagram Adaptation
Carousel flow:
- **Slide 1 (hook/promise):** The result/milestone up front, under 12 words (e.g., "I bought 4 properties using AI-assisted research")
- **Slides 2-3:** The story — context, what happened, compressed
- **Slides 4-6:** The turning point — where things changed
- **Slide 7-8:** The lesson — stated as personal discovery, not prescription
- **Final slide:** "Save this if you're navigating a similar journey" or "What's your version of this story?"
- **Caption:** 150-300 chars. English.

### X Adaptation
Single tweet: the result/milestone + the lesson in one punch. Thread version: Hook tweet (result first) → story in 2-3 tweets (compress ruthlessly) → lesson tweet (personal discovery, not advice) → closing question. Build-in-public angle: share the journey of building or discovering something about the real estate AI space.

### LinkedIn Adaptation
1,300-1,900 chars. Hook in first 210 chars: lead with the result or milestone — narrative opener that creates immediate context. One paragraph per story beat. Structure: hook → backstory (brief) → the story compressed (specific moments, not summary) → the lesson (stated as "I realized..." not "you should...") → what this means professionally. English. End with "Has something changed your thinking about [topic] recently?"

## Scaffold: Rant/Reaction

**Default hook:** Raw rant
**Common alt:** Mid-action story
**Classification:** Something frustrated or surprised you emotionally.

**Flow:**
[hook — emotion first, raw and unfiltered] →
[what happened — the trigger, briefly] →
[raw take — your unprocessed reaction]

**Pacing notes:**
- First line = pure emotion. No context. ("靠 我真的快氣死")
- Context comes second — keep it to 1-2 lines
- End raw. No neat bow. No lesson. Just the feeling.
- This scaffold almost never needs an explicit CTA — the emotion IS the engagement driver

### RED Adaptation
Long-form note — but keep the raw energy. Title captures the emotion with niche specificity (first 18 chars, e.g., "海外買家仲介崩潰日記｜AI到底行不行"). Body: raw hook → trigger explained with enough context for a reader who doesn't know you → your unprocessed reaction in full → optional: "有沒有人有一樣的感覺" as closing. Can be more vulnerable and detailed on RED — KOC storytelling resonates. 600+ chars. Traditional Chinese.

### Instagram Adaptation
Carousel flow:
- **Slide 1 (hook/promise):** The emotion up front, bold visual (e.g., "Nobody talks about how broken this workflow is")
- **Slides 2-4:** The trigger — what happened, briefly. One detail per slide.
- **Slides 5-7:** The raw take — your unprocessed reaction, broken into beats
- **Final slide:** "Tell me I'm not the only one" or leave it raw — no explicit CTA
- **Caption:** Short, 50-150 chars. Match the emotion. English.

### X Adaptation
Single tweet: the frustration or reaction in one raw line, under 280 chars. The emotion IS the engagement. Thread version only if the trigger requires more explanation: Hook tweet (pure emotion) → trigger tweet (what happened, brief) → reaction tweet (raw take) → optional closing question. Don't over-explain.

### LinkedIn Adaptation
1,300-1,900 chars. Adapted for professional context — raw but grounded. Hook in first 210 chars: lead with the frustration stated professionally (not "靠" but still sharp). One idea per paragraph. Structure: hook (the frustration) → trigger (what happened, with professional context) → raw take (what this reveals about the industry) → implicit or explicit invitation to commiserate. English. Don't sanitize it into a lesson — the reaction is the point.

## Scaffold: Question Post

**Default hook:** Curiosity gap
**Common alt:** Pain point
**Classification:** You noticed something and genuinely don't have the answer.

**Flow:**
[hook — observation that something is off] →
["here's what's weird..." — the specific oddity] →
[genuine open question — you actually want answers]

**Pacing notes:**
- This only works if you genuinely don't know the answer. Fake questions = transparent.
- The "weird" detail must be specific — vague oddities don't hook
- End with the question. Don't add your guess — let readers fill the gap.

### RED Adaptation
Long-form note. Title = the observation as a mystery (first 18 chars, e.g., "海外買家仲介｜一個我想不通的現象"). Body: hook with the observation → explain what's weird with specifics (client scenarios, market data, tool behavior) → the open question, stated genuinely. Can include your partial hypotheses — RED readers like to solve problems. End with direct question + hashtags. 600+ chars. Traditional Chinese.

### Instagram Adaptation
Carousel flow:
- **Slide 1 (hook/promise):** The observation that something is off, under 12 words (e.g., "Why aren't more agents using AI for overseas buyers?")
- **Slides 2-4:** "Here's what's weird" — the specific oddity, one element per slide
- **Slides 5-7:** Build the question — context that makes readers lean in
- **Final slide:** The question itself, clean and open. "I genuinely don't know the answer — what do you think?"
- **Caption:** 150-300 chars. End with the question. English.

### X Adaptation
Single tweet: the observation + the question in one tight punch, under 280 chars. Don't provide your answer — leave the gap. Thread version: Hook tweet (something is off) → weird detail tweet → closing question tweet. Works well as conversation starter in PropTech/agent communities.

### LinkedIn Adaptation
1,300-1,900 chars. Hook in first 210 chars: lead with a specific observation about the real estate AI space that begs a question. One paragraph per thought. Structure: hook (the observation) → what's weird about it (specific details from your work or research) → the question (stated openly, no hedging, no guesses). End with the question as the final line — short, clean, direct. English.

## Global Pacing Rules (Apply to All Scaffolds)

### Universal Rules (All Platforms)
- Vary sentence length: short (5-8 chars) → medium (15-20) → short (3-5). Never 3+ similar-length sentences in a row.
- Open loop by line 2 if post exceeds 4 lines.
- Decreasing length toward the end — reading momentum.
- Every line must earn its place. If cutting a line loses nothing, cut it.
- One idea per line/unit — mobile-first pacing.

### Platform-Specific Overrides
- **Threads:** Max 2 lines before a rhythm break (line break, interjection, pivot). Exception: hook openers with intentional repetition. Short/staccato pacing dominates.
- **RED:** Pacing is section-based, not line-based. Headers create rhythm. Body sections can run 3-5 sentences before a break — completeness matters more than staccato.
- **Instagram:** Pacing is slide-by-slide. One idea per slide = one pacing unit. Within a slide, keep to 1-3 short lines. Visual weight = pacing.
- **X:** Pacing is tweet-by-tweet for threads. Single tweets must land in one punch — no internal pacing needed. Every word counts toward the 280-char limit.
- **LinkedIn:** Pacing is paragraph-based. 2-3 sentences per paragraph max. Aggressive line breaks between paragraphs — whitespace is a pacing tool. First 210 chars must hook before "See more."
